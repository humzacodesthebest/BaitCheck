    Spot the scam

    Real scammers bury 3–4 tricks in one short message. Tap every word or phrase you think is a red flag, then check yourself.

    If you already tapped or replied

    1. Stop. Don’t send money, codes, or more messages. Scammers rush you so you skip this step.
    2. Go direct. Open the company’s real app or type its address yourself — never through the link you were sent.
    3. Lock it down. If you entered a password, change it and turn on two-factor authentication.
    4. Report it. Forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM), then delete the message.

    The three tricks behind almost every scam

    Hurry. “Locked in 24 hours,” “offer expires.” Urgency is manufactured so you don’t check.

    Impersonation. A real brand name glued to a fake address — chase-secure-login.xyz is not Chase.

    Untraceable payment. Gift cards, crypto, and wires are hard to recover once sent. Any stranger asking for them is the tell — if you’ve paid, contact your bank, exchange, or card issuer immediately.
